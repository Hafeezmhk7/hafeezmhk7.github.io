Mohammad Hafeez Khan

I work on computer vision, mainly on learned representations of 3D scenes. From 2024 to 2026 I was at the University of Amsterdam, where my main project was an autoencoder over 3D Gaussian Splatting scenes: it compresses a scene into a compact latent and decodes it back. I also built the differentiable rendering pipeline that supervises it in image space, working through camera poses, intrinsics and rendered depth.

At UvA I also worked on detecting AI-generated faces with CLIP and latent reconstruction errors (ACM workshop paper), on a mechanistic-interpretability reproduction study of how language models handle facts and counterfactuals (TMLR), and on multi-modal generative recommendation. Before that, I completed an MSc in Data Science & AI with Distinction at Queen Mary University of London, where my thesis was on machine translation for low-resource languages.

My first degree is a B.Tech in Engineering Physics from NIT Agartala (ranked 2nd in my department). There, and during a summer internship at INST Mohali, I characterised materials with X-ray diffraction and UV-visible spectroscopy, so imaging physics is familiar ground. I prefer problems where the method has to work on real measured data, and I am interested in supervising 3D representations through the imaging process itself rather than on their raw parameters.

CLaRE: CLIP with Latent Reconstruction Errors for Generated Face Detection
Udit Thakur*, Mohammad Hafeez Khan*, Meher Changlani, Asen Dotsinski, Aswin Krishna Mahadevan, Ioannis Kechagias, Ivona Najdenkoska
Proceedings of the 1st ACM Workshop on Deepfake, Deception, and Disinformation Security (3D-Sec), co-located with ACM CCS 2025, Taipei
* equal contribution
paper/ code/

We add latent reconstruction-error (LaRE) features to CLIP through a cross-attention fusion module and learnable prompts (CoOp/CoCoOp) to detect AI-generated faces. On DF40 it reaches 78.3% mean accuracy, 1.5 points above prompt-tuning baselines, with the largest gains on unseen generators such as MidJourney and StarGANv2.

On the Generalizability of “Competition of Mechanisms: Tracing How Language Models Handle Facts and Counterfactuals”
Asen Dotsinski, Udit Thakur, Marko Ivanov, Mohammad Hafeez Khan, Maria Heuss
Transactions on Machine Learning Research (TMLR), 2025
arXiv/ OpenReview/

We reproduce Ortu et al. (2024) on GPT-2 and Pythia 6.9B and test whether its findings carry over. On Llama 3.1 8B, attention-head specialisation is much weaker. Small prompt changes, such as not repeating the counterfactual verbatim, sharply weaken the counterfactual mechanism. The effect also depends on the prompt's domain.

Theses

Semantic Scene Completion with Latent Modeling of Scene-Level 3D Gaussians
Mohammad Hafeez Khan
Master’s Thesis, Artificial Intelligence, University of Amsterdam, Jan–May 2026
Supervisors: Yue Li, Prof. Martin R. Oswald · Examiner: Dr. Shaodi You
thesis (pdf)

This thesis studies the standard two-stage recipe (a VAE that compresses a scene, then a flow-matching generator in its latent space) on thousands of unfiltered indoor 3DGS scenes from SceneSplat-7K. A seven-variant ablation shows that two choices decide whether the autoencoder generalises or memorises: fixed-frame Hilbert ordering of the Gaussians, and a windowed local encoder with a token-local decoder. Together they more than halve validation reconstruction error. A hierarchical layout-then-geometry flow-matching model reproduces the latent structure and recovers coarse layout from partial scans.

The project also showed why parameter-space supervision is ill-posed for 3DGS. A fixed decoder slot sees differently oriented surfaces across scenes, so the L2-optimal output collapses to an isotropic blob. The (scale, quaternion) parametrisation is also gauge-ambiguous, so L2 measures numeric rather than shape distance.

Follow-up: I replaced the continuous latent with a discrete VQ-VAE supervised in image space. I built the depth-masked fine-tuning pipeline end to end: OpenCV-to-renderer pose conversion, normalised intrinsics for a wide-angle capture rig, metric depth rendered with gsplat, and a loader for the raw attribute format. Fine-tuning improved held-out reconstruction from 17.0 to 19.2 dB PSNR, and LPIPS from 0.386 to 0.327.

Transfer Learning Based Multilingual Neural Machine Translation for Low Resource Indic Languages
Mohammad Hafeez Khan

Supervisor: Prof. Shalom Lappin

Effect of Cadmium Sulphide on Electrical and Optical Properties of Polyaniline
Mohammad Hafeez Khan
B.Tech Thesis, Engineering Physics, National Institute of Technology Agartala, Jan–Jun 2020
Supervisor: Dr. Biswajit Saha
thesis (pdf)

Synthesised polyaniline/cadmium sulphide (PANI/CdS) nanocomposites by in-situ polymerisation and characterised them with X-ray diffraction, UV-visible absorption and I–V measurements. The CdS template orders the polymer chains, the absorption bands at 410 and 850 nm confirm PANI's conducting phase, and the composite conducts 13 times better than pure PANI.

Research Experience & Projects

Multi-Modal Semantic Item Identifiers for Generative Recommendation
Mohammad Hafeez Khan
Research project, University of Amsterdam, May–Dec 2025
Supervisor: Kidist Amde Mekonnen
report (pdf)/code

Fused CLIP visual features with Sentence-T5 text embeddings before RQ-VAE quantisation, keeping the TIGER retriever fixed so that any change comes from the visual semantics alone. Across eight Amazon categories (2014 and 2023), images help a lot where appearance distinguishes items (Sports, Toys, Video Games) and little or not at all in text-centric ones such as Software. I also brought beyond-accuracy metrics (coverage, Gini exposure, user-level disparity) into the semantic-ID setting. They reveal a trade-off: broader catalogue coverage can come with more concentrated exposure.

Synthesis and Characterization of NaLa(MoO4)2:Er3+,Yb3+ Nanomaterials and their Upconversion Photoluminescence Properties
Mohammad Hafeez Khan
Summer Research Internship, Institute of Nano Science and Technology (INST), Mohali, Jun–Jul 2019
Supervisor: Dr. Sanyasinaidu Boddu
report (pdf)

Synthesised Er3+/Yb3+ co-doped NaLa(MoO4)2 nanophosphors by room-temperature co-precipitation and characterised their structure and phase purity. Under 980 nm excitation they show green (525, 552 nm) and red (652 nm) upconversion emission, which is strongest at 3% Er3+ and 8% Yb3+.
